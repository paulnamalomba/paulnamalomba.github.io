Level 1 — Role Overview & Opening Strategy

Understand the role inside-out and craft a compelling first impression.

📋 Role Decoded

The CTO — Data Science and IT is a permanent, Payclass 10 role that acts as the department's technical backbone across teaching, research, and system administration. It is split 0.8 FTE in the Department of Civil Engineering and 0.2 FTE in the EBE Faculty Office.

What This Role Actually Is

  • Not a traditional CTO managing executive strategy — it's a hands-on technical officer providing specialist data science and IT services to academics, researchers, and students.
  • Teaching support (40%): Helping roll-out a digital design learning line, supporting coding/data classes, training tutors.
  • Research support (30%): Data collection, database management, debugging code, helping researchers use Python/SQL/R and the HPC cluster.
  • Systems development & sysadmin (20%): Building and maintaining SEAT and other Django/Python tools, Linux server management, LDAP, Apache2.
  • Management (10%): Data repositories, budgets, hardware/software procurement, committees.
Key Insight

The role is deeply operational — they need someone who can do the work, not just manage it. Your hands-on SEAT experience, SESKA development, and day-to-day coding make you ideally positioned.

🎤 "Tell Me About Yourself" — Opening Pitch (2 min)

Script — Adapt to Your Natural Voice

"Thank you, Panel, for this opportunity. I'm Paul Namalomba — I hold a Master's in Structural Engineering with distinction from UCT and I'm currently completing my PhD in Civil Engineering, expected to submit in August this year.

What excites me about this CTO role is that it sits at the intersection of everything I've been building over the past four years right here in this department.

On the systems side, I've spent nearly two years developing and maintaining SEAT — the Faculty's own Student Enrolment Advising Tool. I built the backend in Python Django with PostgreSQL, implemented LDAP security, and innovated on data loading with MPI and hyperthreading to handle 30 million rows efficiently. I improved server availability to 100% with zero errors per month.

On the research computing side, I've spent two and a half years as the lead engineer on SESKA — the department's finite element software suite. I deployed it on UCT's HPC cluster, completed a full Linux-to-Windows port, and work daily in C++, Fortran, Python, and Linux.

On the teaching side, I've been an assistant lecturer for four semesters in this department, training five teaching assistants and consistently achieving 85%+ course pass rates.

I've also earned professional certificates in Data Science from Google, Data Engineering from IBM, and Backend Development from Microsoft — totalling over 750 hours of structured training.

What makes me uniquely suited is that I'm not coming in cold — I already know the systems, the people, the infrastructure, and the needs of this department. I'm ready to hit the ground running from day one."

Key Talking Points to Weave In

  • Continuity: "I already maintain SEAT and SESKA — no ramp-up time needed."
  • Breadth + Depth: Cover all 6 KPAs naturally — teaching, research, systems, management.
  • Quantify: 30M rows, 100% uptime, 85%+ pass rates, 750+ hours training, 500+ GitHub contributions.
  • Forward-looking: "I'm excited about building the Digital Lab vision and strengthening the digital design learning line."

🏆 Your 5 Key Differentiators

These are the points no other candidate can match. Repeat them in different forms throughout the interview.

1. You Already Built SEAT

15 months of hands-on SEAT development. You know the codebase, the architecture, the stakeholders (Celeste Wilson, the faculty), and the operational cycle. Any other candidate would need 6+ months to ramp up.

2. You're Embedded in the Department

PhD student, assistant lecturer, SESKA developer — you know the research groups (CoMSIRU, Centre for Transport Studies, Future Water), the culture, and the people. Prof. Skatulla (your supervisor) is on the panel.

3. Full-Stack Technical Range

From machine-level C++ to web apps in Django/JS to data pipelines to HPC — you cover the entire technical stack this role demands. Most candidates will be strong in one area; you cover all six KPAs.

4. HPC + Linux Expertise

You've deployed software on UCT HPC with Slurm, written OpenMPI code, and administered Linux servers. The JD specifically calls for HPC cluster liaison and Linux competency.

5. Certified Data Science + Engineering Pipeline

Google Advanced Data Analytics, IBM Data Engineering (in progress), Microsoft Backend Development — structured training that directly maps to the role's data science and IT requirements.

⚠️ Pitfalls to Avoid

  • Don't oversell your PhD research — this is a technical support role, not an academic research position. Reference your PhD to show domain knowledge, but pivot quickly to operational contributions.
  • Don't assume the SEAT connection guarantees anything — answer every question as if you're proving yourself fresh.
  • Don't get too technical — the panel includes professors who may not know the difference between Django and Node.js. Use clear, jargon-light language with concrete outcomes.
  • Don't forget the 0.2 FTE faculty component — SEAT is only 20% of the role but it's a critical differentiator. Mention it, but don't let it dominate.
  • Prof. Skatulla is on the panel — he knows your work intimately. He can't advocate for you in the room, so don't rely on his familiarity. Treat him as any other panellist.
