Tips, Tricks & Assessment Strategy

Meta-strategies for Thursday 12 March 2026. Dr. Nnene’s assessment will be practical and hands-on. This page covers how to manage your time, communicate under pressure, handle unknowns gracefully, and deploy domain-specific awareness that distinguishes a CTO candidate from a senior developer.

🎯 Assessment Day Game Plan

Pre-Assessment (Morning)
  • Ensure installed: Python 3.11+, pip, VS Code, Docker Desktop (running), pgAdmin or DBeaver. Have these tabs pre-opened: Wokwi, HiveMQ WebSocket client, Excalidraw. Pre-install libraries: pip install paho-mqtt pandas numpy geopandas matplotlib psycopg2-binary. Test that everything works. Remove surprises.
  • Run: docker pull postgis/postgis:16-3.4 and docker pull timescale/timescaledb:latest-pg16. If Docker is available during the assessment, you can spin up a spatial or time-series database in 10 seconds without waiting for download.
  • Create a folder ~/assessment-prep/ with ready-to-paste code:
    • mqtt_subscriber.py — paho-mqtt subscriber template
    • haversine.py — vectorised Haversine function
    • generate_data.py — synthetic GPS generator
    • timescale_setup.sql — hypertable + PostGIS setup
    • docker-compose.yml — TimescaleDB + Mosquitto stack
    These are not cheating — they’re tools a CTO would have ready. Adapt to the specific problem during the assessment.
  • Skim through the IoT/Edge, Data Engineering, and CTO Architecture pages. Don’t study — just refresh. Focus on: the 6-layer pipeline, the MPI narrative, the domain bridges, and your 3 whiteboard exercises. You should feel calm and prepared, not cramming.

💬 Communication Under Pressure

When You Know the Answer
  • Example: Answer: “I’d use MQTT over HTTP for sensor telemetry.” Evidence: “MQTT’s 2-byte header vs HTTP’s 700+ bytes, at 10 Hz from 100 sensors, saves 17 GB/day.” Trade-off: “HTTP would be simpler for the development team, but the bandwidth cost on cellular networks makes it impractical for South African deployments.” This structure shows depth, proof, and judgment in 30 seconds.
  • Never give a purely theoretical answer. Every response should include “At SEAT/ComputeMore/SESKA, I...” or “When I built Ecoride, I...”. This transforms you from “someone who has read about it” to “someone who has done it”. Practise 5 answers aloud, each anchored to a different experience.
When You Don’t Know the Answer
  • If asked about a technology you haven’t used (e.g., a specific simulation tool), say: “I haven’t worked directly with [X], but based on its architecture — [state what you know] — I’d approach it by [logical first step]. In my experience with [similar tool Y], the pattern was [Z], and I expect it transfers.” This shows intellectual honesty + transferable problem-solving.
  • During the practical assessment, narrate your thought process: “I’m going to start by defining the data schema because the pipeline design depends on it...” This gives the assessor insight into your reasoning even if you don’t finish the task. A CTO is evaluated on how they think, not just the final output.

🌍 African Transport Context Awareness

Dr. Nnene’s research is in African transport systems. Demonstrate that you understand the unique constraints.

Domain-Specific Knowledge
  • In African transport contexts, data is often missing, noisy, or unstructured. Mention strategies: (1) Store-and-forward on MCUs for connectivity dropouts. (2) Forward-fill interpolation for gap-filling time-series. (3) Crowdsourced collection (e.g., GPS data from minibus taxis as a data source). (4) Synthetic data generation for training ML models. Saying “we’ll never have perfect data, so we design for graceful degradation” is CTO-level thinking.
  • South African cellular networks: 4G in urban areas but drops to 2G/EDGE on highways. Power outages cause cell tower failures. Design implications: (1) Use MQTT QoS 1 (not 0) for important data. (2) Buffer on the edge device (ESP32 SPIFFS/LittleFS). (3) Use LoRaWAN for rural sensors where cellular is absent. (4) Batch and compress data before transmission. Every design should have an “offline mode”.
  • UCT Civil Engineering is a research department, not a VC-funded startup. Hardware choices matter: ESP32 (R120) not Raspberry Pi (R2,500) for leaf sensors. Open-source everything: Mosquitto, Kafka, TimescaleDB, Grafana — zero license costs. Cloud hosting on UCT infrastructure or Hetzner SA (cheaper than AWS/Azure for SA data residency). Show that you think about total cost of ownership, not just technical elegance.
  • ~70% of South African commuters use minibus taxis, which are largely unmonitored. This is a major research gap for Dr. Nnene’s team. Propose: “What if we equipped 50 taxis with ESP32+GPS nodes to build the first open trajectory dataset for informal transit in Cape Town? Low cost (R6,000 hardware total), massive research impact.” This shows you understand the domain and can propose innovative, actionable research infrastructure.
  • MyCiTi: Cape Town’s Bus Rapid Transit system. ~40 trunk routes + feeder services. Currently uses EMV tap-on/tap-off for fare collection. Dr. Nnene’s work includes optimisation models for BRT route scheduling, passenger demand forecasting, and fleet allocation. Know that real-time AVL (Automatic Vehicle Location) data and APC (Automatic Passenger Counting) data are the key data streams. Be ready to discuss how you’d architect the ingestion of these.

⚡ Edge-First Design (CTO Differentiator)

Edge Processing Strategies
  • Instead of sending 10 readings/second, apply a change-detection filter on the ESP32: only transmit when the value changes by more than a threshold. For GPS, this means: don’t send if the vehicle hasn’t moved (Δlat and Δlon < 0.0001). Reduces data volume by 60–90% for parked vehicles. Implement as a simple comparison in C++; no ML needed.
  • Run a simple threshold check on the MCU: if acceleration > 3G, it’s a crash or severe pothole. Publish an immediate high-priority MQTT alert (QoS 1) to a separate alerts/ topic, bypassing normal telemetry batching. On a Raspberry Pi gateway, run a TFLite model for more nuanced anomaly classification. This two-tiered approach (simple on MCU, complex on RPi) is elegant and practical.
  • Instead of sending raw 100 Hz vibration data (12 KB/sec per sensor), compute FFT on the ESP32 (yes, the ESP32 can do a 256-point FFT in ~2 ms). Transmit only the dominant frequencies and their amplitudes (50 bytes) every 60 seconds. Bandwidth reduction: 99.6%. This is a deeply impressive CTO-level insight to deploy in the assessment.

⏰ Time Management During the Practical

Assessment Time Strategy
  • Resist the urge to start coding immediately. Read the entire problem statement. Identify: (1) What’s the input? (2) What’s the expected output? (3) What are the constraints? (4) What will they evaluate? Jot down 5 bullet points. This prevents wasted effort on misunderstood requirements.
  • Before coding, sketch a quick architecture on paper. Even if the task is “write a Python script”, drawing the data flow helps you structure the code. Label the inputs, processing steps, and outputs. The assessor may see this and give you credit for systematic thinking even before you write a line of code.
  • Get the simplest working version running as fast as possible. For a data pipeline: hardcode the input path, skip error handling, use basic print statements. Once it works end-to-end, add: error handling, proper logging, edge cases, visualisation. A working simple solution beats an incomplete elegant one.
  • Stop coding. Add comments to key sections. Prepare a 2-minute verbal summary: “Here’s what I built, here’s what I’d add with more time, and here are the trade-offs I considered.” This clean wrap-up is what separates a CTO from a developer — you manage scope and communicate outcomes.

💡 Trade-off Mantras (Memorise These)

A CTO never says “this is the perfect solution.” Always pair a recommendation with its trade-off.

Ready-to-Deploy Trade-off Statements
  • “We could use WebSockets for real-time telemetry from sensors, but MQTT is better suited for the unreliable cellular networks these devices will operate on. MQTT’s QoS levels and last-will testament handle disconnections gracefully. For the browser-based dashboard, WebSockets (via Socket.IO or similar) is the right choice because browsers don’t natively speak MQTT.”
  • “InfluxDB would give us slightly faster raw write throughput, but TimescaleDB gives us full SQL and native PostGIS integration. For a research department that needs spatial + temporal queries and will be used by people who already know SQL, the development velocity and query flexibility of TimescaleDB outweigh InfluxDB’s raw performance edge.”
  • “Edge computing isn’t about replacing cloud — it’s about being smart about what we send. Raw high-frequency sensor data is expensive to transmit and mostly redundant. By filtering and aggregating on the MCU, we reduce data volume by 95% and send only actionable information. The cloud then handles long-term storage, complex analytics, and cross-device correlation.”
  • “We could build a perfectly architected system with Kafka, Spark, and a custom ML pipeline, but that takes 6 months and the research team needs data now. I’d start with MQTT → Python script → PostgreSQL+PostGIS → Grafana. Get data flowing in 2 weeks. Then iteratively add Kafka, TimescaleDB, and ML components as the data volume grows. Pragmatic delivery beats theoretical perfection.”
