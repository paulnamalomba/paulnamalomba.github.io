Last-Minute Revision Sheet

Use this for the final 10-15 minutes before the assessment. Everything here is high-signal and tightly aligned to what Dr. Nnene is most likely to probe.

Key Distinctions

  • Simulation: explore or evaluate system behaviour under assumptions.
  • Optimisation: search for a better choice under objectives and constraints.
  • Simulation-based optimisation: use simulation to evaluate candidate solutions when the real system is complex or stochastic.
  • Agent-based model: represent individual actors and let system behaviour emerge from local rules.
  • Activity-based model: model travel as part of daily activity patterns rather than isolated trips.
  • Multi-objective problem: no single perfect answer; trade-offs matter.
  • Pareto front: improving one objective further usually worsens another.

What to emphasize

  • Problem framing before tooling.
  • Validation and assumptions before numerical confidence.
  • Decision support, not just predictive accuracy.
  • Messy data is normal; pipeline discipline matters.
  • Low-cost, robust systems are often better than elaborate fragile ones.
  • Stakeholder trade-offs are part of the technical problem.

Likely technical vocabulary

High-probability terms: agent-based modelling, MATSim, simulation-based optimisation, genetic algorithms, NSGA-II, transit network design, activity-based demand, GTFS, AFC data, transport emissions, EV policy, big data in transport, transport equity, validation, uncertainty.

Likely question angles

  • Explain a modelling approach simply.
  • Choose between simulation, optimisation, and ML.
  • Handle poor-quality data without overclaiming certainty.
  • Design a practical ingestion and analytics pipeline.
  • Explain trade-offs to non-technical stakeholders.
  • Show how your own background maps into applied transport or infrastructure systems work.

Your fast answer checklist

  • State the problem.
  • Name the method and why it fits.
  • Note one trade-off or limitation.
  • Anchor to one real example from your work.
  • End with the decision or outcome that matters.
Final mindset

You do not need to sound like a transport academic. You need to sound like an applied technical person who can reason clearly about systems, data quality, modelling choices, and implementation trade-offs in a transport-facing environment.
