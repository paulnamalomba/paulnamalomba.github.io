30-Min Mock Interview

This mock interview is designed around the public themes in Dr. Nnene's work. Keep answers to 2-3 minutes. The target is structured reasoning with a practical link to your own systems work.

How to run this

Use a timer. Answer aloud. After each question, check whether you clearly stated the problem, the method, the trade-off, and one concrete example from your own work.

Minute 0-4: Opening and Fit

Question 1

Tell me about yourself and why your background fits a role that sits at the intersection of data, systems, and engineering.

What he is likely testing: Whether you can connect engineering breadth to applied computing instead of giving a disconnected CV summary.

Strong answer shape: civil/structural and computational roots, then backend/data/system delivery, then why that combination is useful in research and operational environments.

Question 2

What kind of technical problems do you enjoy solving most?

What he is likely testing: Whether you naturally gravitate toward real systems problems like modelling, scale, reliability, and messy data.

Minute 4-12: Modelling and Simulation

Question 3

How would you explain the difference between simulation and optimisation to a non-technical stakeholder?

Target themes: simulation explores behaviour under assumptions; optimisation searches for better choices under objectives and constraints; they are often combined when exact analytical methods are too brittle.

Question 4

Why would someone choose simulation-based optimisation instead of an exact mathematical solution?

Target themes: complexity, stochastic behaviour, many interacting actors, large search spaces, multiple objectives, imperfect data, need for realistic evaluation rather than elegant but unrealistic exactness.

Question 5

When is an agent-based model useful, and what are its limitations?

Target themes: heterogeneous agents, local rules, emergent system behaviour, route or mode choice, but also calibration difficulty, data requirements, validation challenges, and computational cost.

Minute 12-19: Data Science and Real Data

Question 6

You inherit a transport or infrastructure dataset with missing, inconsistent, and delayed records. How do you proceed?

Target themes: data audit, quality dimensions, missingness patterns, reconciliation rules, logging assumptions, separating data repair from model logic, and validating downstream usefulness.

Question 7

What does model validation look like when you do not have clean ground truth?

Target themes: triangulation against partial observations, sanity bounds, sensitivity analysis, back-testing, stakeholder validation, and explicit uncertainty rather than false precision.

Question 8

When is machine learning appropriate, and when is it the wrong tool?

Target themes: ML is useful when the predictive mapping is too complex for rules, but not for problems that are primarily about optimisation, causal policy choice, or when interpretability and decision logic dominate.

Minute 19-26: Systems and Decision Support

Question 9

Design a lightweight pipeline for collecting, cleaning, storing, and analysing mobility data from low-cost field devices.

Target themes: edge buffering, message transport, ingestion API or broker, time-series or relational storage, monitoring, replayability, data quality checks, low-connectivity design, cost awareness.

Question 10

How do you communicate trade-offs when user cost, operator cost, emissions, and equity point in different directions?

Target themes: multi-objective framing, scenario comparison, Pareto thinking, decision support rather than pretending there is one universally correct answer.

Question 11

Suppose you had to support both research flexibility and operational reliability in one platform. How would you approach the architecture?

Target themes: separation between experimental and production paths, reproducibility, auditability, modular services, stable schemas, environment isolation, and observability.

Minute 26-30: Closing

Question 12

What would you want to build or improve in your first 6-12 months if you joined a transport-focused technical environment?

Strong answer direction: a credible small platform idea such as a reusable ingestion and analytics stack for transport or infrastructure data, or a clean simulation-support workflow that improves both research throughput and reliability.

Scoring yourself
  • Did you define the problem before naming tools?
  • Did you explain a trade-off rather than pushing a single technology answer?
  • Did you anchor the answer to your own work?
  • Did you sound like someone building decision-support systems, not just writing code?
