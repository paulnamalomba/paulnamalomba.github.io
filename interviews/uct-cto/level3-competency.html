1. Analytical Thinking & Problem Solving

Level 2 The ability to identify problems, analyse information, and develop effective solutions.

Situation

During the SEAT registration cycle, we needed to refresh the student database from PeopleSoft — 30 million rows of student and scheduling data. The existing process was unreliable: it took hours, consumed all server memory, and frequently crashed.

Task

I needed to redesign the data loading pipeline so it could handle the volume reliably, within available server resources, and complete in a reasonable timeframe.

Action

I analysed the bottleneck and identified two issues: the data was being loaded in a single monolithic transaction (memory exhaustion), and it was sequential (CPU underutilisation). I designed a solution using MPI for parallel processing and batch-chunked memory management. Data was divided into manageable chunks, each processed by a separate thread, with memory cleaned between batches. I also implemented validation checkpoints between stages.

Result

The new pipeline is reliable, fast, and resource-efficient. Server availability improved to 100% with zero errors per month during the registration period. This innovation is now a permanent part of SEAT's architecture.

2. Building Interpersonal Relationships

Level 2 The ability to develop and maintain productive working relationships with colleagues and stakeholders.

Situation

In my current roles at UCT, I already work across multiple stakeholder groups: research supervisors (Prof. Skatulla), ICTS technical staff (Theo Armstrong, Sibonelo Twala), the faculty office (Celeste Wilson), departmental administration (Ayesha Dalwai), and students.

Task

Each group has different needs, different technical vocabularies, and different priorities. A researcher wants their code debugged; ICTS wants systems to follow policy; students want things to "just work."

Action

I adapt my communication style to each audience. With academics, I focus on outcomes — "your simulation will run 3x faster" — not implementation details. With ICTS, I speak their language — server specs, compliance, protocols. With students, I prioritise clarity and patience. I also maintain reliability: when I say I'll deliver something by Friday, I deliver by Thursday. Trust is built through consistent follow-through.

Result

I've built a network across the department and faculty that would serve me immediately in the CTO role. Multiple stakeholders — Prof. Skatulla, Celeste Wilson, Ayesha Dalwai, Theo Armstrong — are listed as my referees, which reflects the strength of these working relationships.

3. Client/Student Services & Support

Level 2 The ability to provide quality service and support to students and internal clients.

Situation

During a SEAT registration cycle, students were struggling to identify which courses they still needed to enroll in. The existing interface showed a dense table with limited visual cues.

Task

While not explicitly part of my backend brief, I noticed the pain point and decided to address it.

Action

I designed and implemented new UX components that clearly highlighted outstanding enrolments, using visual indicators and intuitive grouping. I also implemented email automation using SMTP and Django's shell so students could receive personalised notifications about their registration status.

Result

Students found it significantly easier to manage their enrolment, and the faculty office received fewer "Where do I enroll?" queries. It demonstrated my willingness to look beyond my assigned scope when I see a way to improve the user experience.

4. Communication

Level 2 The ability to convey information clearly and effectively, both written and verbal.

Situation

As an Assistant Lecturer, I regularly teach engineering students who are encountering coding and computational concepts for the first time. As a CIO at ComputeMore, I communicate system architecture decisions to non-technical managing directors.

Task

I need to make complex ideas accessible without oversimplifying to the point of inaccuracy.

Action

I use analogies rooted in what the audience already knows. For civil engineering students, I explain database tables as spreadsheets with rules, and APIs as controlled windows into data. I use visual aids — diagrams, flowcharts, live demonstrations. I also maintain a portfolio website with published guides and sample scripts, which serves as reference documentation that people can revisit at their own pace.

Result

93%+ pass rates in CIV1007S across three semesters, and 85%+ in CIV2011F. Students consistently engage with the material, and I've received positive teaching evaluations. At ComputeMore, non-technical leadership can make informed decisions about system architecture because I translate the tradeoffs into business impact.

5. Planning & Organising / Work Management

Level 2 The ability to plan, coordinate, and manage multiple tasks and priorities effectively.

Situation

I currently balance three concurrent roles: PhD research, SEAT development, and assistant lecturing. At ComputeMore, I simultaneously managed two product development projects (Ecoride and INNTEGRATE) while overseeing DevOps.

Task

Each role has different cadences — SEAT peaks during registration (Nov–Feb), teaching runs in semesters, research is continuous.

Action

I use a structured approach: at the start of each semester, I map all deadlines and peak periods across all commitments. I identify conflicts early and plan around them. For daily management, I use task tracking tools and prioritise by impact and urgency. I also build buffer time for unexpected requests, which in a support role are inevitable.

The JD's time split — 10% curriculum, 30% teaching support, 30% research, 10% management, 10% SEAT, 10% sysadmin — provides a natural framework. I'd use that as a baseline and adjust seasonally.

Result

I've maintained high output across all concurrent roles: 100% server uptime on SEAT, consistent teaching pass rates, and a PhD thesis on track for August 2026 submission. This track record shows I can handle the multi-faceted demands of the CTO role.

6. Professional Knowledge & Skill

Level 2 The level of technical and professional knowledge required to perform the job effectively.

Situation

Data science and IT evolve rapidly — tools and best practices shift every 6–12 months. In a university context, I need to balance learning new technologies with maintaining stable, reliable systems.

Action

I take a structured approach to continuous learning. In the past year alone, I've completed professional certificates from Google (Advanced Data Analytics, 300+ hours), IBM (Data Engineering, 150+ hours), and Microsoft (Backend Development, 40+ hours). These aren't just certifications — they include hands-on labs and capstone projects.

I'm also a GitHub Pro contributor with 500+ contributions in the last 6 months, a PyPI package author, and I maintain subscriptions to Google Cloud, AfricasTalking, and other API platforms for hands-on experimentation. I attend conferences like AfriCOMP, ICMM, and SACAM.

Result

Over 750 hours of certified training across data science, data engineering, and backend development — this isn't theoretical knowledge. I apply these skills daily in SESKA, SEAT, and my ComputeMore projects. When a researcher asks about machine learning, I can help because I've recently built models in Scikit-Learn and TensorFlow, not because I read about them five years ago.

7. Safety Awareness

Level 2 Awareness and application of health, safety, and environmental principles in the workplace.

Suggested Answer: "Safety in a CTO Data Science role has two dimensions. First, data safety — protecting sensitive student and research data through proper security protocols, access controls, and backup procedures. With SEAT, I implemented LDAP authentication and SQL injection prevention, and I'd apply these same principles across all departmental systems.

Second, physical safety — when working with IoT devices, sensors, and lab equipment, following proper handling procedures, electrical safety protocols, and university guidelines. When deploying SESKA to computer labs, I coordinated with lab administrators to ensure installations didn't disrupt other systems or create safety hazards.

I also consider ergonomic safety for students spending extended periods at workstations during data-intensive labs, ensuring proper setup and break scheduling."
