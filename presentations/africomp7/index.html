01
Context & Motivation

Why Finite-Strain Viscoelasticity for Ice?

Ice-shelf and near-front dynamics involve transient loading and evolving boundaries that introduce a measurable elastic component alongside long-term creep. This long-term creep effect occurs due to the depth and temperature dependent viscosity of the shelf

Schematic of the ice sheet at the Fimbulisen, depicted in the image is the sea water which is much denser thatn the shelf ice ($\sim 10\%$ denser).

The Glaciological Problem

Quantitative projections of ice-sheet evolution depend on numerical models resolving coupled mass & momentum transport. Calving-front stress redistribution differs substantially between purely viscous and viscoelastic descriptions. (Christmann, 2019)

Review of Existing Approaches

  • Lie/Oldroyd rates of Cauchy-Green tensors correlate to the the 2nd Piola-Kirchhoff stress, which is not a strong descriptor of inelasticity, where a true meterial stress fits better
  • Additional numerical stiffness and compromised algorithmic robustness
  • Constraint drift in $\det\mathbf{F}_v = 1$ over long time horizons

Our Contribution

  • Viscous material velocity gradient $\mathbf{L}_v$ as the flow/evolution rate variable
  • Logarithmic strain measure for nonlinear elastic-viscous effects
  • Exponential deformation map updates preserving $\det(\mathbf{F}_v) = 1$ exactly
  • Glen's Flow Law integrated directly into the formulation
02
Mechanical Framework

Multiplicative Decomposition

The total deformation gradient is multiplicatively decomposed into elastic and viscous parts, introducing a stress-free intermediate configuration $\mathcal{B}_{\text{int}}$:

Multiplicative Split
$$\mathbf{F} = \mathbf{F}_e \, \mathbf{F}_v$$
Right Cauchy-Green Tensor
$$\mathbf{C} = \mathbf{F}^T \mathbf{F} = \mathbf{F}_v^T \, \mathbf{C}_e \, \mathbf{F}_v$$
Elastic Right Cauchy-Green Tensor
$$\mathbf{C}_e = \mathbf{F}_e^T \, \mathbf{F}_e \quad \in \mathcal{B}_{\text{int}}$$

$\mathbf{C}_e$ is naturally associated with the intermediate configuration $\mathcal{B}_{\text{int}}$ and encodes relative elastic deformation in a Lagrangian setting.

Frame Objectivity

Under a superposed rigid rotation on the entire solid body $\mathbf{Q}$:

$\mathbf{F}^* = \mathbf{Q}\mathbf{F}_e \cdot \mathbf{F}_v$

$\mathbf{Q}$ acts only on the elastic part — the viscous deformation and intermediate configuration are unaffected.

Three-configuration motion map: material $\mathcal{B}_0 \xrightarrow{\mathbf{F}_v} \mathcal{B}_\text{int} \xrightarrow{\mathbf{F}_e} \mathcal{B}_t$.

Maxwell viscoelastic solid: elastic spring + viscous dashpot in series.

03
Strain-Rate Tensors

Why the Material Velocity Gradient?

A central modelling decision — choosing the right rate variable determines numerical stability and physical fidelity.

Our Choice — $\mathbf{L}_v$

$$\mathbf{L}_v = \mathbf{F}_v^{-1} \dot{\mathbf{F}}_v \in \mathfrak{sl}^+(3)$$

The material velocity by demand (given the viscous deformation is purely shearing), we will have inelastic effects render as isochoric

  • Volume-preserving: $\text{tr}(\mathbf{L}_v) = 0$ ⇒ purely deviatoric/shearing which corresponds to the fact that $\mathrm{det}(\mathbf{F}_v) = 1$
  • Lie-algebraic: element of $\mathfrak{sl}^+(3)$, enabling exponential map integration for $\mathbf{F}_v$
Conjugate stress to $\mathbf{L}_v$: $\mathbf{L}_v$ is power-conjugate to the material stress $\boldsymbol{\Xi} = \mathbf{C}\mathbf{S}$, yielding $\mathcal{D} = \boldsymbol{\Xi} : \mathbf{L}_v \geq 0$ — a proper dissipation inequality for inelasticity. *Write $\boldsymbol{\Xi} = \mathbf{F}^{-1} \boldsymbol{\tau} \mathbf{F}^{-T}$ instead $\boldsymbol{\Xi} $, as pullback of $\boldsymbol{\tau}$ and this will be proof enough of physical relevance of $\boldsymbol{\Xi}$. $\mathbf{S}$ is the 2nd Piola-Kirchhoff Stress

Proof — Frame Objectivity of $\mathbf{L}_v$

The total material velocity gradient $\mathbf{L} = \mathbf{F}^{-1}\dot{\mathbf{F}}$ is not objective. Under a superposed rotation $\mathbf{Q}$:

$$\mathbf{L}^* = \mathbf{L} + \mathbf{F}^{-1}(\mathbf{Q}^T \dot{\mathbf{Q}})\mathbf{F}$$

where $\mathbf{Q}^T\dot{\mathbf{Q}}$ is the skew-symmetric angular velocity (spin) of the frame. However, substitute $\mathbf{F} = \mathbf{F}_e \mathbf{F}_v$ into the spin term:

$$\mathbf{F}^{-1}(\mathbf{Q}^T \dot{\mathbf{Q}})\mathbf{F} = \mathbf{F}_v^{-1}\!\left[\mathbf{F}_e^{-1}(\mathbf{Q}^T \dot{\mathbf{Q}})\mathbf{F}_e\right]\!\mathbf{F}_v$$

Simultaneously, from the multiplicative split we derive the additive decomposition of $\mathbf{L}$:

$$\mathbf{L} = \mathbf{F}_v^{-1}\mathbf{L}_e\,\mathbf{F}_v + \underbrace{\mathbf{F}_v^{-1}\dot{\mathbf{F}}_v}_{\mathbf{L}_v}$$

Combining, the transformed rate $\mathbf{L}^*$ decomposes as:

$$\boxed{\;\mathbf{L}^* = \mathbf{F}_v^{-1}\!\left[\mathbf{L}_e + \mathbf{F}_e^{-1}(\mathbf{Q}^T \dot{\mathbf{Q}})\mathbf{F}_e\right]\!\mathbf{F}_v + \mathbf{L}_v\;}$$

Result: The rotation-dependent spin terms are entirely absorbed by the elastic component. $\mathbf{L}_v$ is additively isolated and therefore frame-objective — it is unaffected by superposed rigid body rotations $\mathbf{Q}$.

04
Constitutive Theory

Logarithmic Strain & Thermodynamic Consistency

Logarithmic Strain Measure

Defined in the intermediate configuration $\mathcal{B}_\text{int}$:

Hencky Strain
$$\boldsymbol{\alpha} = \ln(\mathbf{C}_e), \quad \mathbf{C}_e = \exp(\boldsymbol{\alpha})$$

$\boldsymbol{\alpha}$ and $\mathbf{C}_e$ share the same eigenvectors $\mathbf{z}_i$, with eigenvalues $\lambda_i = \exp(\epsilon_i)$. For any isotropic energy $\Psi(\boldsymbol{\alpha}) = \Psi(\mathbf{C}_e)$, the key differential identity is:

$$\frac{\partial \Psi}{\partial \boldsymbol{\alpha}} = \mathbf{C}_e \frac{\partial \Psi}{\partial \mathbf{C}_e}$$

Thermodynamic Proof

The Clausius–Duhem inequality requires:

Dissipation Inequality
$$\mathcal{D} = \boldsymbol{\Xi} : \mathbf{L} - \rho_0 \cdot \dot{\Psi}(\mathbf{C}_e) \geq 0$$

Taking the material time derivative and expressing $\dot{\mathbf{C}}_e$ via the elastic-viscous decomposition yields:

$$\dot{\Psi} = 2 \mathbf{F}_v^T \frac{\partial \Psi(\boldsymbol{\alpha})}{\partial \boldsymbol{\alpha}} \mathbf{F}_v^{-T} : (\mathbf{L} - \mathbf{L}_v)$$

Substituting back and applying the Coleman–Noll argument (coefficient of $\mathbf{L}$ vanishes identically):

Constitutive Relation
$$\boxed{\;\boldsymbol{\Xi} = 2\rho_0 \cdot \mathbf{F}_v^T \frac{\partial \Psi(\boldsymbol{\alpha})}{\partial \boldsymbol{\alpha}} \mathbf{F}_v^{-T}\;}$$

As a natural consequence of this constitutive relation, a modified logarithmic strain $\overline{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}$ emerges in the material configuration $\mathcal{B}_0$:

Modified Logarithmic Strain
$$\overline{\boldsymbol{\alpha}} = \mathbf{F}_v^T \boldsymbol{\alpha} \, \mathbf{F}_v^{-T} = \ln(\mathbf{C}\,\mathbf{C}_v^{-1})$$

$\overline{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}$ characterises elastic strains in $\mathcal{B}_0$ and is power-conjugate to $\boldsymbol{\Xi}$. In the small-strain limit $\boldsymbol{\alpha} \approx 2\mathbf{E}$, so Lamé parameters scale as $\lambda \to \tfrac{\lambda}{2},\; \mu \to \tfrac{\mu}{2}$. The reduced dissipation reads:

Reduced Dissipation
$$\mathcal{D} = \boldsymbol{\Xi} : \mathbf{L}_v \geq 0$$

Hyperelastic Constitutive Law (Maxwell Reformulation)

$$\boldsymbol{\Xi} = \frac{\kappa}{2}\,\text{tr}(\overline{\boldsymbol{\alpha}})\cdot\mathbf{1} + \mu\cdot\text{dev}(\overline{\boldsymbol{\alpha}})$$

with the Maxwell viscoelastic balance:

$$\text{dev}(\boldsymbol{\Xi}) = \eta_\text{Glen}\cdot\mathbf{L}_v^T = \mu\cdot\text{dev}(\overline{\boldsymbol{\alpha}})$$

Valid provided $\overline{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}$ and $\mathbf{L}_v$ are co-axial, which holds for the isotropic case.

05
Ice Rheology

Extended Glen's Flow Law

Embedding Glen's creep law directly into the finite-strain material velocity gradient formulation.

Maxwell Reformulation

The Maxwell viscoelastic solid relates deviatoric stress to the viscous strain rate:

Maxwell Constitutive Relation
$$\text{dev}(\boldsymbol{\Xi}) = \eta_\text{Glen} \cdot \mathbf{L}_v^T = \mu \cdot \text{dev}(\overline{\boldsymbol{\alpha}})$$

Glen's flow law introduces a stress-dependent, non-linear viscosity:

Glen-Type Viscosity
$$\eta_\text{Glen} = \frac{1}{2A\,\tau_e^{m-1}}$$
Effective Shear Stress
$$\tau_e = \frac{\|\text{dev}(\boldsymbol{\Xi})\|}{\sqrt{2}} = \sqrt{\frac{\text{dev}(\boldsymbol{\Xi}) : \text{dev}(\boldsymbol{\Xi})}{2}}$$

Final Flow Condition

Viscous Flow Rule
$$\mathbf{L}_v = \sqrt{2}\,A\,\|\text{dev}(\boldsymbol{\Xi})\|^{m-1} \cdot \text{dev}(\boldsymbol{\Xi})$$
Arrhenius Temperature Dependence
$$A = A_0 \exp\!\left(\frac{Q(T_0 - T(h))}{R\,T(h)\,T_0}\right)$$

Arrhenius Parameters

$R = 8.314\,\text{J mol}^{-1}\text{K}^{-1}$ Universal gas constant
$A_0$ Reference rate factor at $T_0$
$T_0$ Reference temperature
$T(h)$ Temperature at depth $h$ in ice
$Q$ Activation energy for ice deformation
$m \approx 3$ Glen stress exponent
06
Numerical Methods

Algorithmic Treatment

Exponential map updates, elastic-viscous predictor-corrector, and consistent linearisation.

1. Trial Strain

Compute $\overline{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}^\text{trial}$

2. Trial Stress

$\boldsymbol{\Xi}^\text{trial}$

3. Viscous Rate

$\mathbf{L}_v^T$ from Glen's law

4. Exp. Update

$(\mathbf{F}_v)_{n+1}$

5. Stress Correct

$\boldsymbol{\Xi}_{n+1},\ \mathbf{S}_{n+1}$

Exponential Map Update

Viscous Deformation Gradient Update
$$(\mathbf{F}_v)_{n+1} = (\mathbf{F}_v)_n \, \exp(\Delta t \cdot \mathbf{L}_v)$$

Incompressibility Preservation

Since $\mathbf{L}_v \in \mathfrak{sl}(3)$, i.e. $\text{tr}(\mathbf{L}_v) = 0$, the exponential map exactly preserves the viscous incompressibility constraint:

$\det(\Delta\mathbf{F}_v) = \det(\exp(\Delta t \cdot \mathbf{L}_v)) = \exp(\Delta t \cdot \text{tr}(\mathbf{L}_v)) = 1$

This is a consequence of the constitutive formulation, the Maxwell-type rheology and the Lie-group structure.

Logarithmic Strain Correction

Elastic-Viscous Corrector
$$\overline{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{n+1} = \overline{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}^\text{trial} - 2\Delta t \cdot \mathbf{L}_v^T$$

Additive correction — a key advantage of the logarithmic strain: finite-strain measures decomposed into additive updates.

Stress Predictor-Corrector

Trial Stress
$$\boldsymbol{\Xi}^\text{trial} = \frac{\kappa}{2}\text{tr}(\overline{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}^\text{trial})\cdot\mathbf{1} + \mu\cdot\text{dev}(\overline{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}^\text{trial})$$
Viscous-Corrected Stress
$$\text{dev}(\boldsymbol{\Xi}_{n+1}) = \text{dev}(\boldsymbol{\Xi}^\text{trial}) - 2\mu\Delta t \cdot \mathbf{L}_v^T$$
Implicit Viscous Rate
$$\mathbf{L}_v^T = \frac{\text{dev}(\boldsymbol{\Xi}^\text{trial})}{\eta_\text{Glen} + 2\mu\Delta t}$$

$\eta_\text{Glen}$ is locally determined from $\|\text{dev}(\boldsymbol{\Xi}^\text{trial})\|$ — no iterative local solve required.

Physical Corollaries

  • Higher strain rate ⇒ higher shear stresses for constant material properties
  • Constant load ⇒ constant strain rate (steady-state creep)

Verification Result

The exponential map update preserves $\det(\Delta\mathbf{F}_v) = 1$ to within $\mathcal{O}(10^{-6})$ across all Gauss points and all load steps — including within the buckling localisation zone.

Example 2 — Ice Sheet Internal Moment Model (Fimbulisen)

Idealised shelf: $L = 6000\,\text{m}$, $H = 120\,\text{m}$. Self-weight + buoyancy loading with depth-dependent material properties.

Boundary Conditions & Loading

  • Hydrostatic pressure load: $p_w = \rho_w \cdot g \cdot h_w$ applied on the submerged right face and bottom boundary, where $h_w \approx 70\,\text{m}$ is the submersion depth
  • Buttressed left edge: roller boundary condition (horizontal displacement constrained, vertical free)
  • Gravitational acceleration: $g = 9.81\,\text{m/s}^2$; body force $\mathbf{b}_\text{ice} = \rho_\text{ice}(z) \cdot g$
  • Pressure distribution: vertical pressure arrows extend uniformly to indicate the same hydrostatic pressure on both the bottom and right-side submerged boundaries

Activation energy @ $T < T_0$, $Q$ $60 \times 10^{3}\,\text{J/mol}$

Depth & Temperature Dynamics

  • Density & Body Force: $\rho_\text{ice} = \rho_\text{ice}(z)$ firn profile; $\mathbf{b}_\text{ice}(z) = \rho_\text{ice}(z)\,\mathbf{g}$
  • Elastic moduli: $\kappa_\text{ice}(z),\,\mu_\text{ice}(z)$ — graded with overburden pressure
  • Viscosity Stratification: The non-linear Glen's flow law incorporates a strong temperature dependence where the rate factor $A(z, T)$ is affected by temperature field variations $T(z)$:
$$\eta_\text{Glen} = \frac{1}{2 A(z, T) \tau_e^{m-1}}, \quad A(z, T) = A_{0} \exp\!\left(\frac{-Q (T_0 - T(z))}{R T(z) T_0}\right)$$

Temperature field variations (Humbert, 2011). This thermal profile $T(z)$ critically governs the viscosity variation $\eta_\text{Glen}$ throughout the ice shelf.

Extended Weak Form

$$\int_\mathcal{B} \mathbf{F}\mathbf{S} : \delta\mathbf{u}\,dV - \int_\mathcal{B} \mathbf{b}_\text{ice}(z)\cdot\delta\mathbf{u}\,dV - \int_{\partial\mathcal{B}_N} \mathbf{t}^{\mathbf{n}}\cdot\delta\mathbf{u}\,dA = 0$$

Stress $\mathbf{S}$ now uses depth-dependent ice moduli $\kappa_\text{ice}(z), \mu_\text{ice}(z)$ and temperature-dependent viscosity.

Uniform Viscosity Example

Numerical results under uniform (non-stratified) viscosity — isolating the purely mechanical response as a baseline for the internal bending moment analysis at the calving front.

Variable (Temperature-Dependent) Viscosity Example

Numerical results with depth- and temperature-stratified viscosity via the Arrhenius-type Glen's flow law. The non-uniform $\eta_\text{Glen}(z, T)$ introduces asymmetric stress redistribution near the calving front, consistent with observed shelf dynamics.

08
Summary

Conclusions & Outlook

Provably Objective Rate Variable

The viscous material velocity gradient $\mathbf{L}_v = \mathbf{F}_v^{-1}\dot{\mathbf{F}}_v$ is additively isolated from all rotation-dependent spin terms under superposed rigid rotations. Within the Maxwell element formulation, $\mathbf{L}_v$ is an objective, symmetric, and isochoric strain-rate measure — replacing higher-order Lie/Oldroyd rates of $\mathbf{C}_e$ that couple poorly to inelastic constitutive behaviour.

Thermodynamic Consistency

A complete Clausius–Duhem dissipation inequality was derived, yielding $\mathcal{D} = \boldsymbol{\Xi} : \mathbf{L}_v \geq 0$. The constitutive relation $\boldsymbol{\Xi} = 2\rho_0 \mathbf{F}_v^T \frac{\partial \Psi}{\partial \boldsymbol{\alpha}} \mathbf{F}_v^{-T}$ emerges naturally from the Coleman–Noll procedure — Glen's flow law is embedded directly without ad hoc overstress potentials or penalty formulations.

Near-Front Bending Dynamics

Both the uniform and variable (temperature-stratified) viscosity simulations capture the expected internal bending moment at the calving front of the Fimbulisen ice shelf. The variable viscosity model, driven by the Arrhenius-type Humbert temperature profile $T(z)$, produces an asymmetric stress redistribution through the shelf thickness — consistent with the depth-dependent creep rates observed in Antarctic field measurements.

Future Work

Extension of the formulation to incorporate damage mechanics — coupling viscoelastic creep with phase-field or continuum damage models to capture crevasse propagation, rift initiation, and eventual calving events at ice-shelf fronts. The Lie-group structure of the exponential map update provides a natural framework for this coupling.
